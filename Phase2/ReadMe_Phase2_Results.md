To validate the numerical setup for the subsequent bluff-body flame holder study, a 2D k-omega SST turbulence model was employed to simulate flow at a Reynolds number of 100,000 over a circular cylinder. To initiate the development of von Karman vortex shedding, 0.001 m/s y-velocity perturbations were introduced to break the initial flow symmetry.

The simulation yielded a Strouhal number (St) of 0.257, representing a 28.5% deviation from the experimental value of St approx 0.2 reported by Achenbach (1981) Additionally, the calculated time-averaged drag coefficient (Cd) was 0.927, showing a discrepancy of approximately 7.3% to 22.8% from Achenbach’s (1968) subcritical range of 1.0 to 1.2.

These discrepancies are well-documented characteristics of 2D RANS simulations at high Reynolds numbers. Unlike the 3D physical experiments conducted by Achenbach, a 2D model cannot account for spanwise vortex stretching or 3D turbulent decorrelation. This forces the vortices to shed in perfectly coherent sheets, which over-predicts the shedding frequency and under-predicts the base pressure suction at the rear of the cylinder.

The mesh consists of approximately 407,000 cells (512,000 max cells for the student version) with an average wall y+ of approximately 0.5, ensuring the viscous sublayer is fully resolved without the use of wall functions. With an average Orthogonal Quality of 0.97 and a minimum of 0.59, and a max Skewness of approximately 0.7, the grid is considered considered sufficiently resolved for the 2D domain given student license constraints. Further refinement would offer diminishing returns, as the current resolution already captures the stable periodic oscillations and boundary layer separation required for the study. Therefore, the simulation process is considered validated for capturing the fundamental unsteady physics of the bluff-body wake in 2-D.
