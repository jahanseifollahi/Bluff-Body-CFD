To validate the numerical setup for the subsequent bluff-body flame holder study, a 2D k-omega SST turbulence model was employed to simulate flow at a Reynolds number of 100,000 over a circular cylinder. To initiate the development of von Karman vortex shedding, 0.001 m/s y-velocity perturbations were introduced to break the initial flow symmetry.

The simulation yielded a Strouhal number (St) of 0.257, representing a 28.5% deviation from the experimental value of St approx 0.2 reported by Achenbach (1981) Additionally, the calculated time-averaged drag coefficient (Cd) was 0.927, showing a discrepancy of approximately 7.3% to 22.8% from Achenbach’s (1968) subcritical range of 1.0 to 1.2.

These discrepancies are well-documented characteristics of 2D RANS simulations at high Reynolds numbers. Unlike the 3D physical experiments conducted by Achenbach, a 2D model cannot account for spanwise vortex stretching or 3D turbulent decorrelation. This forces the vortices to shed in perfectly coherent sheets, which over-predicts the shedding frequency and under-predicts the base pressure suction at the rear of the cylinder.

The mesh consists of approximately 407,000 cells (512,000 max cells for the student version) with an average wall y+ of approximately 0.5, ensuring the viscous sublayer is fully resolved without the use of wall functions. Mesh refinement was concentrated in the wake region under a strict cell-count constraint, prioritizing resolution of vortex shedding over far-field accuracy.The mesh has an average Orthogonal Quality of 0.97 (minimum of 0.59), and a maximum Skewness of approximately 0.7, indicating acceptable mesh quality for the 2D domain.

The simulation captures the qualitative wake dynamics and periodic vortex shedding expected for bluff-body flows. While quantitative discrepancies in Strouhal number and drag coefficient remain, these are consistent with known limitations of 2D RANS modeling at high Reynolds numbers. Therefore, the simulation is considered validated for capturing the fundamental unsteady physics of the bluff-body wake in two dimensions. These results demonstrate an appropriate balance between computational cost and physical fidelity within the constraints of a student-license CFD environment.
